CAROTID BODY 3D CFM PRE-PROCESSING GUIDE (FIJI/ImageJ)
====================================================
Goal: Prepare raw stacks for optimal Ilastik Pixel Classification 
and Frangi Vesselness filtering.

STEP 1: DENOISING (The AI Priority)
-----------------------------------
AI classifiers perform better when edges are not "jittery" from 
camera noise.
1. Open your raw .tif stack.
2. Go to: Process > Filters > Median (3D)...
3. Set X, Y, and Z radius to 1.0. 
   - This removes "salt and pepper" noise while preserving vessel 
     wall boundaries.

STEP 2: ISOTROPIC RESAMPLING (The Geometry Priority)
----------------------------------------------------
Frangi filters calculate "tubularness." If your Z-spacing is 
larger than X/Y, vessels look like pancakes, and the filter fails.
1. Check your current spacing: Image > Properties.
2. Note the 'Voxel Width' and 'Voxel Depth' (Z).
3. Go to: Image > Stacks > Rescale...
4. Set the 'Depth (Z)' scale such that the new Z-spacing matches X/Y.
   - Example: If X/Y is 0.6 and Z is 1.2, set Z-scale to 2.0.
5. Use "Bilinear" or "Bicubic" interpolation for smoothness.

STEP 3: INTENSITY NORMALIZATION (The Consistency Priority)
----------------------------------------------------------
Ensures the AI sees the same "brightness" for vessels across 
different animals.
1. Go to: Process > Enhance Contrast...
2. Set 'Saturated Pixels' to 0.3%.
3. Check 'Normalize' and 'Process Entire Stack'.
4. Go to: Edit > Selection > Select None (to ensure full image).
5. Go to: Process > Math > Min-Max (if available) or use 
   Process > Contrast > Reset to map the range to 0-255 (8-bit) 
   or 0-65535 (16-bit).

STEP 4: FRANGI VESSELNESS GENERATION
------------------------------------
Generate the structural channel that guides the AI toward tubes.
1. Go to: Plugins > Process > Frangi Vesselness (or similar 
   Vesselness plugin).
2. Set 'Number of Scales': 3 to 5.
3. Set 'Minimum Scale': Roughly the width of your smallest capillary.
4. Set 'Maximum Scale': Roughly the width of your largest artery.
5. Save the output as a separate .tif file (e.g., "Animal1_Frangi.tif").

STEP 5: PREPARING FOR ILASTIK
-----------------------------
Ilastik can take multiple files, but it is often easier to 
provide one multi-channel stack.
1. Open your Denoised/Normalized image AND your Frangi image.
2. Go to: Image > Color > Merge Channels...
3. Assign the Raw Image to 'C1' (Red) and Frangi to 'C2' (Green).
4. Save the result as an OME-TIF or HDF5.
5. In Ilastik, ensure both channels are selected as 'Features'.

PRO-TIP: If you find vessels are "hollow" or like "Swiss Cheese," 
increase the Frangi 'Maximum Scale' or use 'Process > Filters > 
Gaussian Blur (3D)' with a radius of 1.0 before the Frangi step.
